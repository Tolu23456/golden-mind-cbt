# Study and career skills

A computer is not only a place to chat and watch videos. Used well, it is a study partner, a library, a workshop and a door to work.

## Study with the computer

### Find good sources

- Start with your textbook and class notes.
- Use official sites, libraries and universities for facts.
- Compare two or three sources before you trust a claim.
- Note the author and the date.

### Take notes that work

Do not copy whole pages. Write the idea in your own words, then add one example. Use headings, short lists and your own diagrams. A good note answers: what is it, why does it matter, and how do I use it?

### Revise actively

- Turn your notes into questions and test yourself.
- Use the Practice Test on this site for computer topics.
- Explain a topic out loud to a friend.
- Space your revision over days instead of cramming one night.
- Mix topics in one session; it feels harder but sticks better.

### Manage your time

1. Write down every task and its deadline.
2. Break big work into 20-minute steps.
3. Do the hardest step first, when you are fresh.
4. Put the phone in another room for one focused block.
5. Check your work against the instructions before you submit.

> A plan you actually follow is better than a perfect plan you ignore.

## Build a simple CV

A CV is a one- or two-page summary of who you are and what you can do.

- **Name and contact** at the top. Use a professional email address.
- **Profile** — two lines about what you are studying and what you want.
- **Education** — school, dates and results.
- **Skills** — typing speed, office programs, coding languages, languages spoken.
- **Experience** — jobs, volunteering, clubs, competitions. Say what you did and what changed.
- **Referees** — people who can speak about you, with their permission.

Keep the design clean: one font, clear headings, no photo unless the employer asks. Save as PDF before sending.

## Apply for opportunities

- Read the instructions twice. Many applications fail on small rules.
- Tailor the CV to the role. Use the same words as the advert where honest.
- Write a short cover message: why this role, why you, what you will bring.
- Check spelling and file names.
- Send before the deadline, not at the last minute.
- Keep a record of what you applied for and when.

## Learn continuously

Free and low-cost ways to keep growing:

- Follow a structured course, like the ones on this site.
- Practise typing until it is automatic.
- Learn one new tool properly instead of ten badly.
- Build small projects and keep them in a portfolio folder.
- Ask for feedback and act on one point at a time.

## Digital footprint

Employers and schools search for candidates. Check your public profiles. Remove anything cruel, illegal or private. Use a sensible profile photo. What you post at fifteen can still be found at twenty-five.

## Try it

Write a one-page CV in your word processor. Include a profile, your education, three skills and one activity. Save it as a PDF, then ask a teacher to mark one thing you should improve.
