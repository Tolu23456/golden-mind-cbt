# Internet and email

The **internet** is a huge network of computers. The **web** is the part of it we open in a browser. Email is one of the oldest and still most useful services on the internet.

## Browsers and addresses

A browser is a program such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari. You type an address into the bar at the top. An address has parts:

- `https://` — the secure way to talk to a website.
- `www.example.com` — the **domain name**.
- `/contact` — the path to a particular page.

The padlock and `https` mean the connection is encrypted. They do **not** mean the site is honest. Scammers can buy padlocks too.

## Tabs, bookmarks and history

- **Tabs** let you keep several pages open. Press [kbd:Ctrl] + [kbd:T] for a new one.
- **Bookmarks** save a page so you can return later.
- **History** lists pages you visited. [kbd:Ctrl] + [kbd:H] opens it.
- **Incognito** or private mode stops the browser keeping local history, but your school or internet provider may still see traffic.

## Searching well

Put the most important words first. Use quotes for an exact phrase and a minus sign to remove a word.

```
"CBT exam" practice -advertisement
```

Check who wrote the page, when it was updated, and whether another trusted site says the same thing. A confident headline is not proof.

## How email works

An email address has two parts:

- The **user name**, such as `ada`.
- The **domain**, such as `school.edu.ng`.

So `ada@school.edu.ng` is read as "ada at school dot edu dot ng".

Every message needs:

1. **To** — the main person you are writing to.
2. **Subject** — a short, clear summary. Never leave it blank.
3. **Body** — your message.
4. **Attachment** — a file sent with the message, shown by a paperclip.

## Writing a good message

- Start with a greeting: "Dear Mr Bello," or "Hello Ada,".
- Say why you are writing in the first sentence.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Be polite. Without a face or voice, words can sound harsh.
- End with "Regards," or "Thank you," and your name.

> Use **Reply** for the sender, **Reply all** only when everyone needs the answer, and **Forward** when you are passing the message to someone new.

## Cc and Bcc

- **Cc** (carbon copy) sends a copy to someone who should know but does not need to act.
- **Bcc** (blind carbon copy) hides the address from other readers. Use it for large groups so you do not share everyone's email address.

## Attachments and file size

Most email services limit attachments to about 25 MB. A big photo album or video may be too large. Upload it to cloud storage and send the **share link** instead.

## Email safety

- Do not open attachments from people you do not know.
- Check the sender's full address, not just the display name.
- A bank will never ask for your password or PIN by email.
- If a message says "urgent" and asks you to click a link, slow down.
- Log out on shared computers.

Write an email to a teacher with the subject "Request for my test score". Keep it under 100 words, include your full name and class, and attach a blank practice file.
