# Passwords and cyber security

**Cyber security** is the habit of keeping your accounts, money and private information safe online. Most attacks do not break the computer. They trick the person using it.

## Make a strong password

A strong password is long and hard to guess.

- Use at least 12 characters.
- Mix words in an unusual way: `goldenMangoRiver42`.
- Avoid your name, birthday, phone number, school or favourite football team.
- Never use `password`, `123456` or `qwerty`.
- Use a different password for every important account.

A short sentence is easier to remember than a random jumble and much stronger than one word.

> Length beats complexity. `purpleBicycleEatsRice` is stronger than `P@ssw0rd!`.

## A password manager

A password manager creates and remembers long passwords for you. You only memorise one strong master password. The browser can also suggest and store passwords, but protect the device with a screen lock.

## Two-step verification

**Two-step verification (2FA)** asks for something extra after your password, such as a code from your phone. Even if someone steals your password, they cannot get in without the second step.

- Prefer an authenticator app or a passkey over SMS codes.
- Keep backup codes somewhere safe and offline.
- Turn on 2FA for email first. Email is the key to resetting everything else.

## Phishing and scams

**Phishing** is a fake message that pretends to be from a bank, school or friend. It wants you to click a link, open an attachment or send information.

Warning signs:

- Urgency: "Your account will close in 24 hours".
- A strange sender address or a misspelled domain.
- A link whose address does not match the name shown.
- A request for your password, PIN or one-time code.
- Poor spelling and grammar, or a greeting like "Dear customer".
- An attachment you did not expect.

If you are unsure, do not click. Open the official app or type the address yourself and check your account there. Call the organisation using a number you already know.

## Public Wi-Fi and shared computers

- Avoid banking on free public Wi-Fi.
- Use a trusted network or your mobile data.
- On a shared computer, use a private window and always log out.
- Never let a browser save your password on a shared machine.

## Malware and updates

**Malware** is software that harms or spies. It often arrives as a "free" download, a cracked program or a fake update.

- Install apps only from official stores or websites.
- Keep your operating system, browser and antivirus updated.
- Do not plug in unknown USB drives.
- Back up your files, because ransomware can lock them.

## If something goes wrong

1. Change the password immediately from a different device.
2. Turn on or reset 2FA.
3. Tell a trusted adult, teacher or the organisation.
4. Check bank messages and report strange transactions.
5. Run a virus scan and disconnect if the device behaves oddly.

> Reporting quickly is not a sign of failure. It is the fastest way to limit the damage.

## Try it

Take three of your accounts and give each a long, unique password idea. Write down which one you will protect with 2FA first, and explain why.
