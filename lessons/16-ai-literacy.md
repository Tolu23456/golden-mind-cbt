# AI and chatbots

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is software that finds patterns in large amounts of data and uses them to make a guess or produce something new. A **chatbot** is a program you talk to in ordinary language.

AI is already in your life: search suggestions, maps, photo filters, voice assistants and the "smart" reply button on your phone.

## What AI is good at

- Summarising a long text.
- Suggesting a first draft or an outline.
- Explaining a hard idea in simpler words.
- Translating, checking grammar and changing tone.
- Writing small pieces of code.
- Generating an image from a description.

## What AI cannot do

- It does not **know** things the way a person does. It predicts likely words.
- It can invent facts, names, dates and quotations. This is called a **hallucination**.
- It may be out of date.
- It can repeat unfair stereotypes from its training data.
- It cannot feel responsibility. You are still responsible for what you submit or publish.

> Never treat an AI answer as proof. Check important facts with a textbook, a teacher or a trusted website.

## Writing a useful prompt

A prompt is your instruction. Better prompts give better answers.

- Say who you are: "I am a first-year student in Nigeria."
- Say the task: "Explain photosynthesis."
- Say the level: "Use simple English and one everyday example."
- Say the format: "Give me five bullet points and one quiz question."
- Add rules: "Do not use words I would not know."

Then improve the answer step by step rather than accepting the first reply.

## Schoolwork and honesty

Using AI to brainstorm or check your grammar can be helpful. Submitting AI text as your own work is usually cheating. Follow your school's rules. Keep your own voice, and be ready to explain every idea.

AI makes mistakes confidently, so "the chatbot said so" is never a reference.

## Privacy

Do not type passwords, bank details, home addresses, other people's personal information or private photos into a chatbot. Many services store conversations and may use them for training. Turn off chat history if the service allows it.

## Bias and fairness

If a model learns from data that favours one group, its answers may be unfair. Facial recognition, hiring tools and exam grading have all shown bias. Ask who is missing from the data and who is affected by the decision.

## AI and jobs

AI will change many jobs. The people who do well will be those who can ask good questions, check answers and combine tools with human judgement. Learn the basics of the tool, but also learn to think clearly.

## Try it

Ask a chatbot to explain a topic from this course in three sentences for a younger student. Then check every fact against your lesson. Write down one thing it got right and one thing it simplified or got wrong.
