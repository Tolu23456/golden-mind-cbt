# Communicating online

Most of our talking now happens through screens: messages, email, group chats, video calls and comments. The same rules of kindness and clarity apply, but the screen removes the face and the voice. That makes misunderstanding easier.

## Choose the right channel

- **A quick question** — chat or text.
- **A formal request** — email.
- **A complicated or emotional conversation** — call or meet in person.
- **A group decision** — a group chat with a clear question, then a summary.
- **A file to keep** — email or cloud storage, not a disappearing chat.

## Writing clearly

1. Say what you need in the first line.
2. Give the important details: what, when, where, who.
3. Keep paragraphs short.
4. End with a clear next step.

Compare "Can we talk?" with "Can we talk for ten minutes about the project at 4 pm today?" The second one respects the other person's time.

## Tone

Text has no facial expression. A short reply can feel rude even when you did not mean it.

- Use greetings and please/thank you in email.
- Avoid sarcasm with people you do not know well.
- Do not use all capitals: IT LOOKS LIKE SHOUTING.
- Explain a joke or soften a correction.
- Re-read before sending. Would you say it to the person's face?

## Group chats

- Stay on topic.
- Do not share someone's phone number or screenshot a private message.
- Do not send the same message five times.
- Be careful with "reply all" and with forwarding.
- If a thread becomes a argument, move it to a call.

## Video calls

- Test your microphone and camera before an important call.
- Mute when you are not speaking.
- Look at the camera when you speak.
- Choose a quiet, well-lit place and a plain background.
- Do not do private things on camera; assume you can be seen and heard.
- Type questions in the chat if the host asks for them.

## Kindness and safety

- **Think before you post.** Screenshots last longer than moods.
- Do not share someone's photo without permission.
- Do not join in when others mock a person. Silence can look like agreement.
- Block and report abuse. Save evidence if you need to show a trusted adult.
- If someone is in danger, tell a teacher, parent or the police. Do not try to handle it alone.

> Behind every username is a real person. Write as if they are sitting beside you.

## Email etiquette

- Clear subject line.
- Short greeting.
- One topic per message.
- No giant attachments without warning.
- Reply within a day or two when you can.
- Use "Reply all" only when everyone needs the reply.
