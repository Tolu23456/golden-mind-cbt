# Make a great presentation

A **presentation** is a set of slides used to explain an idea to a group. PowerPoint, Google Slides and Canva Slides all do the same job: help people understand and remember.

## Start with a plan, not a slide

Before you open the program, answer three questions:

1. **Who** is listening, and what do they already know?
2. **What** is the one message they should remember?
3. **Why** should they care?

Write your main points on paper first. A deck built from a clear plan is always better than a pretty deck built from nothing.

## One idea per slide

A slide is not a document. If a slide needs a paragraph, split it into two slides.

- Use a short title that says the point: "Water is running out", not "Introduction".
- Keep bullet points to about six words each.
- Six lines and six words per line is a good maximum.
- Put the detail in your voice, not on the screen.

## Words and numbers

- Use at least 24-point text so the back row can read it.
- Choose one clear font for titles and one for body text.
- Do not use all capitals for long lines; they are hard to read.
- Keep numbers to two or three per slide.

## Images and colour

A good picture can replace a paragraph. Use one large image instead of four small ones. Keep faces and text in focus, and never stretch an image out of shape.

Pick two or three colours and use them consistently. Dark text on a light background is safest. Remember that some people cannot tell red from green, so do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning.

> Every image needs **alt text**: a short description for people who use a screen reader.

## Charts

Use a chart only when it makes a number easier to see. A single number is clearer as a big statement: "87% of students passed". A trend over time belongs in a line chart.

## Presenting

- Practise out loud at least twice.
- Arrive early and test the projector, sound and internet.
- Stand where you can see the screen and the audience.
- Make eye contact with a few people, not the back wall.
- Speak slowly and pause after important points.
- Do not read the slides. The audience can read.
- Prepare for questions, and it is fine to say "I will find out and get back to you".

## Saving and sharing

Save the original file and also export a **PDF**. The PDF keeps the layout safe if the venue computer has a different version of the program. If you must present online, check that the slides work with the meeting tool before the day.

## Try it

Make five slides about a place in Nigeria. Slide 1: title. Slide 2: a map or photo. Slide 3: three facts. Slide 4: one chart or big number. Slide 5: a closing thought. Add speaker notes instead of long text.
