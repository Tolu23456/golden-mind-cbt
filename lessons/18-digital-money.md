# Digital money and mobile banking

**Digital money** is money you move using a phone or computer instead of cash. In Nigeria this includes mobile banking apps, USSD codes, transfer apps, cards and mobile wallets.

Digital money is convenient, but a mistake can be expensive. Learn the steps before you need them.

## The main ways to pay

- **Bank app** — send money, buy airtime, pay bills.
- **USSD** — a short code such as `*737#` that works without data.
- **Card** — tap, insert or pay online with the 16-digit number.
- **Transfer app or wallet** — a service that holds a balance for you.
- **Direct debit** — a company takes a fixed amount on a schedule.

## Before you send money

1. Check the **account number** and **bank**.
2. Check the **name** the app shows. If the name is wrong, stop.
3. Check the **amount** and the **fees**.
4. Read the confirmation screen before you press Send.
5. Save the receipt.

A transfer that has left your account is very hard to reverse. Treat every Send button as final.

## Protect your account

- Never share your **PIN**, **password**, **OTP** or card CVV.
- A real bank will never ask for your full PIN or a one-time code by phone, SMS or WhatsApp.
- Do not let anyone "help" you at an ATM while you enter your PIN.
- Cover the keypad. Check for loose card readers.
- Use the official app from the store, not a link in a message.
- Turn on transaction alerts so you see every movement.
- Log out after use and lock your phone with a code.

## Common scams

- "Your account is blocked, click here to reactivate."
- "I sent you money by mistake, please refund it."
- A "buyer" who overpays and asks for the difference.
- A job or loan that asks for an upfront fee.
- A caller who already knows your name and last digits of your card.
- A QR code or link that promises free data.

> If it is urgent, secret and asks for money or codes, it is almost certainly a scam. Stop and verify using a number you already know.

## Budgeting with digital money

Digital payments leave a record. Use it. Once a week, list what you spent. Split spending into needs, wants and savings. A simple plan: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings. Adjust to your real life.

Automatic transfers to a savings account on payday make saving easier than promising to save what is left.

## If your phone or card is lost

1. Call the bank from another phone and block the card or app.
2. Change your passwords from a safe device.
3. Check recent transactions.
4. Report to the bank in writing and keep a copy.
5. Report fraud to the police if money was taken.

## Try it

Open your bank app in practice mode or write the steps on paper: send N1,000 to a friend. List every screen you would see and every detail you must check before pressing Send.
